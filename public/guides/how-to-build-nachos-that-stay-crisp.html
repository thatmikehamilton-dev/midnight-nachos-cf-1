How to Build Nachos That Stay Crisp to the Last Chip

Soggy nachos are a construction problem, not a chip problem. Here is the order to build them in, what to keep off the tray until the end, and how hot the oven should be.

By Midnight Nachos Kitchen · Published November 16, 2026 · 2 minute read

The short version

Use sturdy chips in a single overlapping layer, put cheese directly on the chips, bake hot and fast, and add anything wet after the tray comes out. Two thin layers beat one tall pile.

Why nachos go soggy

Chips soften for two reasons: water and steam. Water comes from salsa, beans, tomatoes and sour cream sitting on the chips. Steam comes from piling everything so high that the middle of the heap cooks in its own moisture.

Every fix below is a way of keeping water off the chips until you are ready to eat.

Start with the right chip

Thin restaurant-style chips are lovely with salsa and hopeless under toppings. Pick a thicker chip, the kind that can scoop without snapping. If the chips have been open for a while, five minutes in the oven on their own will dry them out and bring the crunch back.

Build in this order

  1. Chips, in one layer with the edges overlapping. Use a sheet pan, not a deep dish.
  2. Cheese, straight onto the chips. Melted cheese forms a barrier that protects the chip from what goes on top.
  3. Warm, fairly dry toppings: seasoned meat that has been drained, beans that have been drained, pickled jalapeños patted dry.
  4. A little more cheese to hold those toppings in place.
  5. Bake.
  6. Cold and wet toppings last: salsa, sour cream, guacamole, fresh tomato, lettuce, lime.

If you want more nachos, make a second layer of chips and cheese on top of the first before baking. Do not just pile one layer higher.

Cheese notes

Grate it yourself if you can. Bags of pre-shredded cheese are coated with an anti-caking powder so the shreds do not clump, and that coating makes the melt a little grainier. It still works. Block cheese simply melts smoother.

A blend is better than one cheese. Something that melts well, such as Monterey Jack, plus something with more flavour, such as sharp cheddar, covers both jobs.

Oven, not microwave, when you can

A hot oven, around 400°F (200°C), melts the cheese in five to ten minutes and dries the chips slightly as it goes. Watch the edges, because chips go from toasted to burnt quickly.

A microwave melts the cheese but steams the chips. If it is the only option, use a plate rather than a bowl, work in a single layer, and heat in short bursts.

Serve the wet things on the side

The most reliable method is also the least glamorous: put salsa, sour cream and guacamole in small bowls and let people dip. The tray stays crisp for as long as it lasts, which admittedly is not long.
