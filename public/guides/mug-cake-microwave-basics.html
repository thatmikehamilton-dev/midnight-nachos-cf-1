Why Your Mug Cake Turned Out Rubbery, and How to Fix It

The four usual reasons a microwave mug cake or brownie fails, how to adjust for your microwave’s wattage, and a simple way to test without wasting ingredients.

By Midnight Nachos Kitchen · Published November 16, 2026 · 2 minute read

The short version

Rubbery mug cakes are overcooked, overmixed or carrying too much egg. Stir only until combined, use a large mug, cook in short bursts, and stop while the centre still looks slightly wet.

What is happening in the mug

A microwave heats the water in the batter. The cake sets as that water turns to steam and the flour and egg firm up. This takes about a minute, which leaves almost no margin. Ten extra seconds can be the difference between soft and chewy.

The four usual causes

  • Overcooking. By far the most common. The cake keeps cooking from its own heat after the microwave stops.
  • Overmixing. Stirring develops gluten in the flour, which makes the crumb tough. Mix until you cannot see dry flour, then stop.
  • Too much egg. A whole egg is a lot for one mug and makes the result spongy. Many good mug recipes use no egg at all, or only the yolk.
  • Not enough fat. Butter or oil keeps the crumb tender. Cutting it to save calories produces a bouncy cake.

Adjust for your microwave

Recipes are usually written for a microwave of about 1,000 to 1,100 watts. The wattage is printed on a label inside the door or on the back of the oven.

Your microwaveWhat to do
700 to 900 wattsAdd 15 to 30 seconds, in short bursts.
1,000 to 1,100 wattsFollow the recipe, but check early.
1,200 watts or moreReduce the time, or cook at 70 to 80 percent power.

Lower power for slightly longer gives a more even result than full power for a shorter time.

Use a bigger mug than you think

Batter rises to roughly double its height and then settles. A mug filled more than halfway will overflow. A wide mug also cooks more evenly than a tall, narrow one.

How to test without wasting a batch

  1. Cook for about two thirds of the time the recipe gives.
  2. Look at the surface. It should be set at the edges and still slightly glossy in the centre.
  3. Continue in ten-second bursts, checking each time.
  4. Stop while the very centre looks a little underdone, and let it stand for a minute.

Write down the time that worked. It will be the same next time in that mug and that microwave.

One more thing

Let it cool for a minute or two before eating. The inside of a mug cake is much hotter than the surface suggests, and molten sugar burns.
